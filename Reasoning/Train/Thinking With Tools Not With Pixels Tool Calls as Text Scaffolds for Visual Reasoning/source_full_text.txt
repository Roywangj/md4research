                                         1. Introduction
                                         Tool-augmented vision-language models (VLMs) increasingly think with images: they call crop, zoom, or
                                         code tools and reason over the returned pixels [103, 63, 21, 47, 75, 23]. This “thinking-with-images” (TWI)
                                         paradigm—a form of multimodal reasoning that interleaves language generation with visual tool use—
                                         has driven steady gains on perception-heavy benchmarks and is now the engineering default at industrial
                                         scale [40, 45, 18]. Yet a growing set of diagnostics challenges the premise that returned images carry the
                                         reasoning signal. At the most direct level, replacing returned images with noise drops V*Bench accuracy
                                         by only 0.52 percentage points (pp) [31], removing images at inference even raises MathVista accuracy by
                                         3.5 pp [16], and only 57% of returned crops actually contain the target object [22]. More structurally, over
                                         70% of post-RL gains arise from intrinsic capability rather than tool use [35], and text-only self-calling chains
                                         of thought already outperform their pixel-interleaved counterparts [88]. Taken together, these diagnostics
                                         leave the positive mechanism unresolved: if returned pixels are not carrying the gain, what signal is?
                                            We hypothesize that the missing causal signal is the structured text the model emits before any returned
                                         pixel arrives: the tool name, target coordinates, target description, and call intent (e.g., “crop the lower-left

it into reasoning text (intent and target description) and spatial code (crop coordinates) shows that both
components matter, though coordinate grounding matters more on some benchmarks than others.
    These three angles converge on the Tool-Call Scaffold Hypothesis: in current thinking-with-images
distributions, the load-bearing signal is the structured text emitted at tool-call time, while the returned image
is often only the carrier being replaced. In practice, TextCall preserves accuracy while reducing per-sample
latency by 29–46%, eliminating tool-execution overhead, and removing multi-turn image-token injection
from the architecture.
   Our contributions are as follows:

   • We introduce a carrier-swap ablation that isolates returned pixels from the tool-call scaffold and tests
     pixel necessity across LoRA, full fine-tuning, and RL settings.
   • We diagnose why the swap works with a paired scaffold-vs-image audit and content-decomposition
     ablations that identify which scaffold components matter.
   • We instantiate the principle as TextCall, achieving parity-or-better accuracy with 29–46% lower latency,
     zero tool-execution overhead, and no multi-turn image-token injection.

    We scope our claims to current thinking-with-images training distributions and the evaluated benchmark
suite; future tasks may make returned pixels genuinely load-bearing rather than redundant. This boundary
points to a concrete benchmark need: settings where the scaffold alone cannot substitute for the visual
return.

2. Related Work
Thinking-with-images: pixels as the unit of visual thought. Thinking-with-images (TWI) was introduced
at industrial scale by OpenAI o3/o4-mini [40], whose chain-of-thought natively crops, zooms, and rotates
input images via tool calls. Canonical crop-and-zoom TWI systems share an implicit assumption: the returned
pixel aids subsequent reasoning—the pixel return is the unit of visual thought. Earlier SFT-based manipulation-
chain systems—CogCoM [42] with intrinsic visual operations and Chain-of-Spot [32] with ROI-conditioned
re-encoding—already operationalized this pixel-return assumption before the RL-driven wave. V* [78]
introduces a visual search tool, DeepEyes [103] adds crop-based search with RL, and DeepEyes V2 [21] scales
the recipe with a cold-start corpus and multi-turn agentic RL [67, 71, 92]; Pixel Reasoner [63] extends pixel-
space operations with curiosity-driven RL; ViGoRL [47] combines MCTS-grounded trajectories with GRPO. A
wave of concurrent systems further expands the design space with interleaved vision-language reasoning,
reward shaping for tool use, and domain-specific adaptations [54, 77, 70, 26, 24, 65, 62, 5, 96, 97, 14, 98, 79].
The paradigm has also reached industrial scale: Qwen3-VL [45] and GLM-5V-Turbo [18] train native
zoom/crop toolsets via cold-start SFT and tool-integrated RL. An adjacent line distills or bypasses the
returned pixel altogether: Zooming without Zooming [75] collapses multi-turn zoom into a single forward
pass, and Seg-Zero [30] routes text-only positional prompts to an external segmentation model whose mask
outputs are not re-encoded into the reasoning stream—a concurrent RL instance of decoupling tool calls
from pixel feedback; see Su et al. [55] for a survey. Our work tests whether the pixel-return assumption
holds by training without it.


Diagnoses of pixel necessity. Prior evidence challenges the pixel-as-unit reading from several angles (full
table in Appendix H). Inference-time interventions show that returned pixels carry surprisingly little causal
weight: replacing crops with noise drops accuracy by only 0.52 pp while corrupting text drops it by 13 pp [31],
removing images can even raise accuracy [16], over 70% of post-RL gains arise from intrinsic reasoning
rather than tool use [35, 5], and only 57% of crops contain the target object [22]. Training- and data-side
evidence corroborates: original-image attention rewards recover most gains without tool-output signal [41],
zoom benefits can be distilled into single-pass inference [75], lighter spatial mechanisms such as implicit
re-focus tokens [86] and bbox-coordinate reasoning chains [13] achieve grounding without pixel returns,
zero-vision SFT activates visual reasoning without visual data [25], and text-only self-calling CoT outperforms
pixel-interleaved iMCoT [88]. Meanwhile, divergent repair strategies—aligning visual actions [87], reducing
blind invocations [82, 62], step-level verification [106]—improve performance through orthogonal levers,
further suggesting that the role of returned images remains poorly isolated. What is missing is a matched
training-time carrier swap: models that never see image feedback during training, removing the train–test
mismatch confound that clouds inference-time diagnostics. TextCall provides this counterpart and isolates
the role of the textual scaffold.


The faithfulness gap in language-space reasoning. A parallel line of work asks whether LLMs and
VLMs truly reason over their decoded traces. Chain-of-thought faithfulness studies show that reasoning
traces frequently diverge from the model’s internal decision process [60, 27, 6, 91]; in VLMs specifically,
image-based biases influence answers yet rarely surface in CoT [46, 53, 57, 2], RL training improves
accuracy while degrading CoT faithfulness [100], and thinking-mode reasoning amplifies text bias rather
than strengthening visual grounding [73]. Visual tokens become redundant after early layers [3, 94], and
latent-space alternatives show reasoning need not even be decoded [20, 64, 1]. From the training side,
textual slow-thinking data transfers to multimodal reasoning [10], though extended chains risk “visual

anchor drifting” [56, 61, 11]. Unlike latent-reasoning work, we study the decoded tool-call scaffold emitted
before the visual observation: our claim is that this observable scaffold is the load-bearing signal under a
controlled carrier swap.


Structured text as the reasoning medium. Structured intermediate text—chain-of-thought [74, 38],
self-consistency extensions [69, 104, 89], reasoning-trace RL [8], and multimodal CoT [99, 49, 81]—has
been the locus of capability gains in both LLMs and VLMs. MM-CoT [93] benchmarks whether visual CoT is
grounded in images; Thinking in Space [84] identifies the spatial reasoning boundary where textual CoT
fails. Our work extends this thread to tool-augmented VLMs, providing the training-time ablation that prior
CoT studies lack.


Tool calls as actions vs. as thought primitives. Tool-augmented LLMs traditionally treat the tool call as an
action whose returned observation drives the next step [90, 48, 58, 19, 33, 50]. Adjacent visual-prompting
work shows that Set-of-Mark’s symbolic mark identifiers enable GPT-4V to reference spatial regions in its
textual output [83]. VisionThink [85] finds that 50% of pixel tokens can be skipped without loss—evidence
for selective pixel redundancy. Concurrent work also explores alternative carriers between text and re-
encoded pixels: v1 [7] learns to copy visual token embeddings back into the reasoning stream rather than
re-encoding cropped images. These results, together with counterfactual studies probing whether reasoning
depends on its ostensible substrate [34, 80, 37], suggest a re-interpretation of the tool call as a thought
primitive—a structured token sequence whose value may be largely independent of the observation it triggers.
Our pixel-return ablations provide direct training-time evidence for this re-interpretation within the TWI
paradigm.


3. TextCall Matches or Exceeds Thinking-with-Images
Tool-call training improves VLMs by teaching them to emit structured calls that localize and query visual
evidence. We ask whether this gain requires the returned pixels, or whether the pre-return tool-call scaffold is
sufficient. Prior diagnostics mostly remove or corrupt tool outputs only at inference time, leaving a train–test
mismatch: the model was trained to expect images but evaluated without them. TextCall answers the
training-time version of this question: it preserves the tool call and execution confirmation, but replaces every
returned image with the fixed sentinel “[Image output skipped]” during both training and inference.
Across SFT scales, TextCall matches or exceeds thinking-with-images on the core suite. Under GRPO, TextCall
maintains active tool use at the reported checkpoint; the matched thinking-with-images RL run collapses to
direct answering and is analyzed separately as training-dynamics evidence.

3.1. TextCall as a Training-Aligned Carrier Swap

In standard thinking-with-images training, the model receives two coupled signals: it learns to emit a
structured tool call, and then it consumes the image returned by that call. TextCall keeps the first signal
intact and changes only the second. After the tool executes, the returned image is replaced with “[Image
output skipped].” The model still generates the full tool call instruction and observes the execution
confirmation, but never receives the visual output. Critically, this is applied during training—the model is
never trained to expect images. See Figure 1.

4. Why TextCall Works: The Scaffold Carries the Gain
Having shown that the pixel carrier can be removed without reliable loss (Section 3), we now ask what
remains when the image return is gone. Two complementary analyses address this: a paired audit that de-
composes image and scaffold contributions in the training corpus, and a controlled decomposition experiment
identifying which textual components of the scaffold are load-bearing.

executable .crop() calls, but under TextCall these produce no visual feedback, resulting in hallucinated
spatial attention—the model acts as if it observed a region it never saw. Removing code while preserving
reasoning also degrades performance on average (−6.81 pp mean), though the effect is benchmark-dependent:
HR-4K shows no degradation (reasoning only is +0.62 pp above the full scaffold; reasoning alone suffices
for high-resolution search), while V*Bench (−9.94 pp) and CV-2D (−11.12 pp) require code grounding to
anchor spatial reasoning to concrete coordinates.
   The ordering full scaffold > reasoning only > code only reveals a compositional structure: reasoning
provides the why (which region matters and what to look for), code provides the where (specific coordinates
that anchor the reasoning), and their combination forms the effective scaffold. Neither component alone
recovers full performance. The full table (Table 7) including a skeleton-only baseline is in Appendix C.
   Appendix F provides a small Visual Jigsaw cross-check; we treat it as supporting evidence rather than an
independent benchmark claim.

5. TextCall Is the Lower-Cost Default When Pixels Are Not Load-Bearing
The Tool-Call Scaffold Hypothesis yields two deployment-relevant consequences (Table 4).


Within Agent Mode, TextCall matches or exceeds thinking-with-images at lower deployment cost.
Beyond preserving 6-bench accuracy (+1.39 pp; §3.3), TextCall eliminates image-returning tool executions
and reduces end-to-end latency by 29–46%. The latency gap is architectural: each returned image incurs
∼498 ms of vision-token prefill in subsequent turns, whereas sandbox execution contributes only 15% of
total latency. Under the same decode-only sensitivity model used in Figure 4, TextCall’s savings grow
monotonically with decode speed: at the measured 543 tok/s anchor, the canonical sandbox deployment
saves 29% and the latency-only floor saves 46%; doubling decode speed pushes the latency-only savings
toward ≈ 50%, as the fixed per-image vision-prefill cost dominates thinking-with-images inference.


Deployment default. For tasks where the structured spatial prompt captures the task-relevant evidence,
TextCall should be the default because it preserves accuracy while removing returned-pixel overhead. Retain
pixel returns when the task plausibly has a representational bottleneck or a visual prior gap, such as novel
objects or fine-grained visual differences not well captured in text. Meta-cognitive policies that allow the
model to decline tool use are a complementary mitigation [82]. Extended boundary conditions under which
pixel returns may become load-bearing are discussed in Appendix G.

B. Data Audit Details
The headline statistics in Section 4 come from a four-condition factorial audit over all N = 1,000 sampled
DeepEyesV2 SFT trajectories (500 perception + 500 computation, seed 42). A Gemini-3-Flash judge
(temperature 0) answers the same question under four input conditions: question only, question plus original
image, question plus structured spatial prompt without image, and question plus both image and prompt.


Trajectory parsing. Each trajectory is parsed into the user question, the original image, the first assistant
reasoning block before code execution, the code blocks that specify crop/zoom operations, and the final
answer. The structured spatial prompt used in the audit is restricted to the first <think> block and code
blocks. It excludes tool execution results, later reasoning, and the final answer. We verified that the final-
answer literal appears in fewer than 1% of scaffold code blocks (5 out of 1,000 samples), indicating that
direct answer copying is not driving the scaffold-only result.



                                                                                                                      20
                                         Thinking With Tools, Not With Pixels



Judge conditions. The question-only condition gives the judge only the question. The image-only condition
adds the original image. The scaffold-only condition adds the structured spatial prompt but withholds the
image. The combined condition gives both the image and the prompt and serves as the reference. The
resulting accuracies are 51.20%, 73.50%, 73.10%, and 79.40%, respectively.


Paired uncertainty. We compare scaffold-only against image-only with paired bootstrap resampling over
the same N = 1,000 samples. The difference is −0.40 pp with 95% CI [−3.20, +2.40], which passes a 5 pp
non-inferiority margin. Thus the structured spatial prompt recovers equivalent accuracy to the original image
under this audit protocol.


Gain decomposition. Relative to the question-only baseline, the combined condition gains 28.2 pp. Of this
total gain, 57% is redundant accuracy gain recovered by both image and scaffold, 22% is image-unique, and
21% is scaffold-unique. The paired overlap is: both correct 62.8%, image-only 10.7%, scaffold-only 10.3%,
and neither 16.2%.


Caveats. The audit is descriptive mechanism evidence, not the primary causal claim. The causal claim is
established by the training-time intervention in Section 3.3; this audit explains why that intervention can
preserve accuracy under the evaluated training distribution.

D. TextCall Return-Text Ablation
Our TextCall intervention replaces every returned image with the literal text [Image output skipped]
and changes nothing else in the trajectory. This fixed-sentinel return policy keeps the model’s tool-call
scaffold intact (the model still produces crop/zoom arguments, code, search queries, etc.) while removing
the post-call pixel carrier.


No caption or oracle information enters training. All TextCall runs reported in Table 1 (TextCall at 9.5K
LoRA, TextCall at 65K full FT, TextCall SFT+RL) use the same fixed placeholder: 100% of samples replace
the returned image with [Image output skipped]. We never provide captions of returned images or
ground-truth-derived answer hints, so the reported carrier-swap results isolate the fixed text sentinel from
caption-return or oracle-return alternatives.


What this ablation does not address. The fixed-placeholder intervention cannot tell us whether a higher-
quality external caption would have helped the model, nor whether oracle hints would have closed the
residual gaps on benchmarks where neither thinking-with-images nor TextCall reaches state-of-the-art.
We treat such positive-evidence experiments as future work, consistent with the limitations discussed in
Section 6.


Execution policies and latency measurement. The canonical TextCall accuracy rows execute the same
agent loop as thinking-with-images, but the observation returned to the model is always the fixed text
placeholder rather than a new image. The latency-only no-execution row in Table 4 is a separate reference:
the model still emits tool-call text, but the sandbox call is skipped after generation. This row estimates the
pure inference lower bound and is not used for accuracy comparisons.

G. Extended Boundary Discussion
As a hypothesis-level extension to the limitations in Section 6, two candidate conditions may make returned
pixels load-bearing: (i) a representational bottleneck where the visual evidence cannot be losslessly verbalized,
and (ii) a prior-knowledge gap concentrated in visual pretraining and poorly represented in text corpora.
The benchmarks in our suite do not appear to jointly stress both conditions strongly. We expand here on
candidate task families where these conditions might become active; verifying whether thinking-with-images
then outperforms TextCall on such tasks remains future work.


Tasks requiring visual simulation. Wu et al. [76] demonstrate that visual generation can unlock spatial
reasoning that text-only training cannot, particularly for tasks requiring the model to mentally simulate
physical transformations (e.g., rotating, folding, or intersecting objects). These tasks may satisfy both
conditions: the spatial relationships are hard to describe verbally (bottleneck), and the dynamics are learned
from visual experience (knowledge gap).


Tasks requiring fine-grained visual comparison. Design and construction tasks require the model to
compare subtle visual differences across intermediate states. If the differences exceed the granularity of
natural language—e.g., slight curve variations in architectural drawings, or minor alignment offsets in
mechanical assemblies—both conditions could be met: the differences are hard to verbalize (bottleneck)
and the comparison skill is rooted in visual experience (knowledge gap).
   We emphasize that these are hypotheses, not conclusions. Verifying whether generated images provide
genuine causal benefits on such tasks—using the same training-time intervention methodology—is a necessary
next step. Our TextCall framework provides the experimental tool: if TextCall training matches thinking-
with-images on these candidate tasks, returned pixels are still not empirically load-bearing under the tested
protocol; if thinking-with-images significantly outperforms TextCall, the result would indicate that at least
one candidate condition is active and should be analyzed directly. See Section 5 for the corresponding
deployment recommendations.

but it is not direct proof of the optimization pathway. One plausible interpretation is that, during RL
exploration, the model finds a direct-answer policy with comparable reward to tool-augmented paths under

contrast, the TextCall checkpoint is trained and evaluated with the scaffold-only carrier, making active

J. Coordinate Precision Within the Code Body
Section 4.2 shows that removing the code body degrades V*Bench by 9.94 pp and CV-2D by 11.12 pp,
establishing that the syntactic structure of .crop() calls is load-bearing. This leaves open whether the
code component works by providing accurate numeric spatial evidence or by imposing an explicit structured
region-selection cue regardless of coordinate accuracy. We test this with a coordinate-precision ablation:
matched models trained on correct versus shuffled coordinates.


Design. We render 3,317 verified training samples into matched arms differing only in the spatial evidence
within the assistant reasoning: correct coordinates (real bounding-box values from verified object proposals),
shuffled coordinates (values randomly permuted across samples—syntactically valid .crop() calls with
spatially meaningless arguments), and free CoT (no coordinates or spatial markers; descriptive reference only,
not matched to the Section 4.2 code-removal ablation). All arms train Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct [45] with LoRA
rank 8 for 3 epochs and are evaluated under Direct MC think-mode with heuristic scoring on four benchmarks
(HR-4K, MMStar, CV-2D, CV-3D). Three seeds (42, 43, 44) are run for the primary correct-vs.-shuffled
comparison.


Results. Table 11 reports the cross-seed means. Correct and shuffled coordinates are statistically equivalent:
the item-level mean difference across all 14,806 paired predictions (3 seeds × 4 benchmarks) is −0.11 pp
(90% CI [−0.74, +0.51]), passing a paired-bootstrap TOST equivalence test at δ=2 pp (p=0.006). The
equivalence margin is less than one-third of the 9.94 pp code-removal effect in Section 4.2. At the per-
sample level, the two models produce identical predictions on 87.6% of instances; on V*Bench (seed 42,
191 samples), concordance reaches 90.6% with a symmetric discordant split (8 vs. 7, McNemar p=1.0). A
single-seed replication on the weaker Qwen2.5-VL-7B [44] base is consistent with the same pattern (correct
minus shuffled: +0.13 pp).

Scope. The 3K multi-seed test establishes equivalence; the 9.5K matched-scale run shows the same quali-
tative pattern, arguing against a scale-specific artifact. An important caveat: both experiments operate at
the supervised fine-tuning stage, where the base model has already acquired spatial understanding from
vision–language pre-training. Coordinate precision may well matter during pre-training, where grounding
data teaches the model what spatial coordinates mean in the first place (e.g., the bounding-box pre-training
stages in Qwen2.5-VL [44] and similar models). Our finding is specific to the post-training regime: once a
model already possesses spatial grounding from pre-training, the coordinate values in fine-tuning data serve
as a structural cue rather than a source of new spatial knowledge.

of spatial code (having .crop() calls at all) matters substantially, while the numeric content (whether
coordinate values are instance-correct) does not detectably matter. This pattern is consistent with the code
body acting as a structured procedural cue that requires the model to specify a region before continuing its
reasoning, rather than serving as a numerically accurate spatial reference.
